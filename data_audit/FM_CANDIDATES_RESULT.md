## 2. Judging conventions (recorded in each JSON under `judging_conventions`)

Points the investigator may want to review:
* **Secondary C3 (generic video generative model)** includes video tokenizers, VAEs and masked video autoencoders with
  a pixel-reconstruction objective (the VideoMAE family). V-JEPA and V-JEPA 2 fail C3 by their authors' own wording
  ("a non-generative model").
* **C4 (documented pretraining data)** requires a model card or paper that names the video datasets or sources.
  Generic descriptions ("internal", "web-scale") fail. This excludes Cosmos-Tokenize1, SVD, CogVideoX, HunyuanVideo,
  Wan, LTX, Mochi and others.
* **Primary Cosmos-H models** pass C4 on their named surgical training data, although the base Cosmos data are generic
  (the base models fail C4 in the secondary survey).
* **Appendix300 rule:**
  * Basis: the training data of the released weights plus the initialisation, base or frozen backbone.
  * **NO** only for closed lists of named datasets that exclude it.
  * **UNKNOWN** for YouTube / WebSurg / web-scale / open stock-footage sources, which cannot exclude the source
    recordings.
  * No documentation mentions DS203 or Appendix300.
  * The OPARA release (issued 2026-04-29) is later than most releases, so the open question is only prior circulation
    of the recordings.
* **Dresden links (not Appendix300):** EndoViT, SurgeNet and SurgeNetXL include DSAD, robot-assisted rectal surgery
  from University Hospital Carl Gustav Carus Dresden, which is one of the Appendix300 institutions but a different
  procedure and dataset.

## 5. Embedding-compute estimates (`lead/fm_compute_estimate.py`, `.csv`, `.json`)

* **Method:** compute = (inputs per hour of video) × (FLOPs per input) / (sustained throughput).
  * **FLOPs per input:**
    * documented where available (VideoMAE / VideoMAE V2, OmniTokenizer, LeanVAE);
    * transformers: 2 × params × tokens;
    * standard CNN backbones: standard GFLOPs (2 × GMACs);
    * convolutional video VAEs: per-parameter-per-voxel cost calibrated from VidTok's documented 10.35 TFLOPs at 157M
      params and 17 × 256² (**order of magnitude only**).
  * **Inputs per hour:** the model's documented sampling. Where only a frame stride is documented, a 25 fps source is
    assumed; DS203 is 25 fps at centres 2–3 and 50 fps at centres 4–5.
  * **Sustained throughput:** 30 % of the dense BF16 peak derived from NVIDIA's quoted peaks:
    * RTX 4500 Ada: "634.0 TFLOPS" FP8 Tensor (sparse) → 158.5 dense BF16 → **47.6 TFLOPS sustained**;
    * RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Workstation: "4000 TOPS" FP4 (sparse) → 500 dense BF16 → **150 TFLOPS sustained**.
* **Results:**
  * Frame encoders at 1 fps and short-clip video encoders (EndoViT, SurgeNet, EndoMamba, LemonFM, EndoSSL, VideoMAE,
    LARP, LeanVAE): **under 0.05 GPU-hours per video-hour**, i.e. **under about 5 GPU-hours for all of DS203**
    (145.6 h) on the RTX 4500 Ada.
  * Convolutional video VAEs at full temporal density: tens of GPU-hours for DS203.
  * Surgical world / generative models: Cosmos-H 2B ≈ 18–180 GPU-h on the 4500 Ada; Cosmos-H 15.2B and KVLR 12B: 150–375
    GPU-h on the 4500 Ada, about 3.2× less on the PRO 6000.
* **Memory:**
  * the **15.2B Cosmos-H-Surgical v0.3.1** and the **12B KVLR** do not fit in 24 GB in BF16 (offload or quantisation
    needed);
  * everything else fits on both GPUs.
* **Not included:**
  * Video decoding: 22.0 M native frames over 145.6 h; one-fifth of the hours are 4K. Decoding is likely the dominant
    cost for the small models.
  * I/O from LaCie.
